{/* topic:start voice */}
## Voice

Write clearly and directly for the person using the product. Prefer familiar language to
internal terminology, and use the same voice across related experiences.
{/* topic:end voice */}

{/* topic:start tone */}
## Tone

Adjust tone to the situation without changing the underlying voice. Be concise for routine
actions, reassuring when something goes wrong and specific about what the person can do next.
{/* topic:end tone */}

{/* topic:start grammar-mechanics */}
## Grammar and mechanics

Use sentence case for interface labels and headings. Name actions with specific verbs, use
punctuation consistently and avoid filler that does not help the reader.
{/* topic:end grammar-mechanics */}

{/* topic:start inclusive-language */}
## Inclusive language

Use respectful, gender-neutral language and avoid assumptions about a person's ability,
identity or circumstances. Prefer expressions that remain clear when translated.
{/* topic:end inclusive-language */}

{/* topic:start terminology */}
## Terminology

Choose one name for each important concept and use it consistently. Define unfamiliar product
terms near their first use, and keep a shared glossary as the vocabulary grows.
{/* topic:end terminology */}
