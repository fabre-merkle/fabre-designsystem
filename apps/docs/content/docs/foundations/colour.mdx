## Colour strategy

Colour makes a product recognisable, directs attention, and signals meaning. Keep these jobs
distinct so brand expression never undermines comprehension.

The brand defines a small set of colour families. Interface roles describe what a colour does,
such as identifying an action, a reading surface or a warning. A role keeps its purpose when
the brand or mode changes, so meaning remains consistent across experiences.
{/* topic:end colour-strategy */}

## Roles and hierarchy

- **Brand families** express identity. Primary, secondary and tertiary describe their roles in
  the brand, not the priority of actions or controls.
- **Neutrals** provide structure through surfaces, text, icons and boundaries. They support
  readability and allow deliberate accents to stand out.
- **Utilities** communicate shared meanings: error and destructive intent, warning, success
  and information.

Brand hierarchy and interaction hierarchy are separate decisions. Choose interaction emphasis
for the task rather than inferring it from a brand family's name. Never rely on colour alone
to communicate a state; support its meaning with text, an icon or another visible cue.
