## Depth model

Depth shows how parts of an interface relate: what belongs together, what sits on top and
what demands attention. A surface's depth is relative to the surface beneath it.

**Surface level** distinguishes a surface's fill and edge from its surroundings. **Shadow**
indicates that it floats. Keeping the two ideas independent lets content nest clearly without
automatically accumulating heavier shadows. The page is the base and casts no shadow. Light
and dark modes express depth differently while preserving the relationships.
{/* topic:end depth-model */}

## Depth in action

Move the surface to explore its relationship to the surrounding page. The controls start
linked to compare matching levels as a preview aid, not a component rule; unlink them to
adjust surface and shadow independently. The sharing panel and permission menu start open so
you can compare all three surfaces while their shadows stay independent. The example does not

## When to raise or recess

Raise a surface to show that it sits above its surroundings: a card groups content, a menu
floats temporarily and a dialog takes focus from what lies beneath. A nested surface rises
from its immediate surroundings, not from the page.

Recess a region to show that it sits within a surface, such as a code block or a group of
inputs. A recess is a dip, not negative elevation, and needs no shadow. Use shadow
deliberately; deeper nesting should not automatically mean a heavier shadow. Avoid adding
layers without a clear reason.
{/* topic:end elevation-usage */}
